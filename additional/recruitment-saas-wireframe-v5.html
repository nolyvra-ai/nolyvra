AI-Generated Validation Questions
1
Risk Consistency
Your CV shows a 4-month gap in early 2019 that does not appear on your LinkedIn — can you walk me through what you were doing during that period?
▸ Why this question?
Timeline discrepancies between CV and LinkedIn are a top authenticity signal. A gap present on one document but absent on another requires explanation — it may indicate contract work, personal circumstances, or embellishment.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Clear, specific account of the period (e.g. freelance work, study, personal leave). Consistent with any other references provided. No deflection or vagueness.
2
Risk Skill Inflation
Your CV lists Kubernetes as a skill but your own summary describes it as "learning" — what is your actual production experience with K8s today?
▸ Why this question?
Internal contradiction within the same CV document is a high authenticity risk. Candidates sometimes list technologies they are aware of rather than have production experience in. The JD requires this skill.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Specific cluster configurations used. Deployment strategies (Helm, ArgoCD). Honest acknowledgement of learning stage with concrete progression evidence.
3
Technical Capability Gap
The role requires event-driven architecture — describe a system you designed or built using Kafka, RabbitMQ or a similar message broker.
▸ Why this question?
Event-driven architecture is explicitly listed in the JD as a requirement and is absent from the candidate's CV entirely. This is a weighted capability gap that accounts for 25% of the scoring matrix.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Named a specific system, described producer/consumer design, handled failure scenarios (DLQ), discussed throughput or ordering guarantees.
4
Domain FinTech
This role is in a regulated fintech environment — describe your exposure to compliance requirements such as PCI-DSS, GDPR, or financial audit trails.
▸ Why this question?
Domain knowledge is the lowest-weighted but highest-risk gap. FinTech Co. operates under financial regulation. Compliance-naïve engineers create audit and security risks.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Named specific regulatory frameworks. Described how it shaped system design decisions. Aware of data residency, masking, or audit logging requirements.
5
Architecture Deep Dive
You led a monolith-to-microservices migration — what was the scale of the system, how did you sequence the migration, and what would you do differently?
▸ Why this question?
This is a top strength signal but also an authenticity probe. Genuine architects can articulate sequencing, tradeoffs, and failure learnings. Candidates who overstate ownership struggle to answer this in depth.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Described strangler fig or similar pattern. Identified specific domain boundaries. Articulated team coordination challenges and data migration strategy.
6
Behavioral Leadership
Tell me about a time you had to push back on a technical decision made by senior stakeholders — what was the situation and what was the outcome?
▸ Why this question?
Mentorship claims are easy to make. This behavioral probe tests whether the candidate operates with genuine Architect-level influence and psychological safety — both critical in the JD's collaborative culture requirement.
▸ Strong Answer Signals
Specific scenario with named stakes. Used data or prototypes to make the case. Remained constructive when overruled. Shows both confidence and professional maturity.
